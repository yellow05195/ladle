# SECURITY

Status on 2026-10-28: **deployed on devnet only, not on mainnet-beta; not audited.** An earlier build (v22, 393,128 bytes) runs on devnet and played a 2-player round end to end (`tests/devnet/play-round.ts`); this build (v24, the second review's fixes) runs end to end on LiteSVM (`tests/cu-bench`, windows of 1 to 2048 entries) and is to be upgraded onto devnet next. The owner's plan (`docs/LAUNCH.md`): a short devnet test, then mainnet-beta with the program **still upgradeable** (§2) and no audit. This file states what the program can and cannot do, what was revoked by design, where the trust lies, who holds which key and what each key allows (§7), and what remains unverified.

## 1. What the program can never do (by construction, checked by `tests/idl-authority.ts`)

| power | status |
|---|---|
| move a stake out of escrow other than to the curve + fees at settlement, or back to its owner on refund | **does not exist** |
| cancel or modify an entry after `seal` | **does not exist** (no instruction) |
| change the fee rate, the roll law, the per-entry cap, the window length, the VRF timeout after creation | **does not exist**; constants are `pub const`, market fields are written only in `create_market` |
| pause, freeze or gate `sell_back`, `sell`, `refund_window`, `refund_entry` | **does not exist**; no flag, no authority, only the owner's signature where the owner receives funds. Quote mints that would give a third party that power are refused at `create_market` (§5: classic SPL Token only, no freeze authority) |
| mint more of the launched token | **revoked**: mint authority set to `None` after the single initial mint; freeze authority `None` |
| take part of the draw | **does not exist**; the only revenue paths are the fixed market creation fee and the 1.00 % platform fee on the window total, both hard-coded |
| choose the draw's outcome | **does not exist** for the program, the creator, the keeper or participants, unless ORAO's fulfilment authorities collude (§3). Whoever sends the first `request_vrf` picks its salt, which picks the seed ORAO is asked for, not the outcome: ORAO's answer to a new seed is unknown until it lands on chain |
| decide between "draw" and "refund" after seeing the value | **does not exist**: `refund_window` fails if the ORAO request is fulfilled, `fulfill` fails once refunding, and the refund only opens after `vrf_timeout_slots` |

The instruction set is closed: `create_integration, create_market, open_window, enter, seal, request_vrf, fulfill, tally_page, settle_page, refund_window, refund_entry, sell_back, sell, close_entry, close_window`. Fifteen. Nothing else. Everything but the user-signed money paths is permissionless. There is no `claim` since v25: LADLE never leaves the market's vault to a wallet, so a share is sold back (by its owner, or for them at a shutdown, below) and never has to be chased in wallets. The **wind-down build** (§2) is another program build: it has none of `create_integration, create_market, open_window, enter`, and three instructions the normal build does not have: `start_wind_down, force_exit, close_market`.

The two cleanup instructions only return rent. `close_entry` closes an Entry whose share is final and fully sold back (or whose stake is refunded) and sends the rent to `entry.owner`, whoever calls it. `close_window` closes a finished Window and its escrow and sends the rent to `window.opener`: a Settled window at once, a Refunding window only once every stake is refunded; the market's active window too once finished (the market then has no active window, and the next `open_window` needs no previous one: since the second review, the last round of a market no longer keeps its rent) (`refunded_entries == entry_count`; the refunds are paid from the escrow, signed by the window). Quote found in the escrow at that point can only have been sent there from outside; it is swept into the market's quote vault, so dusting an escrow cannot block the close. Neither can move a stake or a share, and neither pays the caller.

**Exits outlive the window (v22).** A player who never sells back must not keep the keeper's window rent locked, so nothing a player needs lives in the Window: `enter` copies the window's `market` and `index` into the Entry, `settle_page` sets the Entry's `settled` flag with its `share`, and `sell_back`, `close_entry` and `force_exit` read only the Entry and the Market. Invariants, each checked on the SBF build by `tests/cu-bench` against a closed window:

- *Only after the whole window settled.* `sell_back` and `force_exit` require `entry.settled && entry.window_index < market.next_settle_index`. The cursor moves past a window only when it becomes Settled or Refunding, strictly in index order, never back; `settled` is written only in a Tallied window, which can never become Refunding. So this is exactly "the window is Settled", whether its account still exists or not (a sale between two settle pages is refused).
- *Only from its own market.* `has_one = market` on the Entry: the vaults are the market's whose `next_settle_index` was read; another market's vaults are refused (`ConstraintHasOne`).
- *Only by its owner, only once.* The owner signs `sell_back` (`has_one = owner`); `claimed` (the part sold) only grows and is capped at `share`, so a sell-back with nothing left gets `NothingToClaim`. `sell_back` and `sell` take a `min_quote_out` the seller signs: sales that land first and move the price past it make the sale fail (`SlippageExceeded`) instead of paying less (the site passes its quote minus 1 %). An Entry closes only with nothing left (`settled && claimed == share`, or `refunded`), its rent to `entry.owner` only, and its address cannot come back: `enter` is the only instruction that creates it and needs the window Open, and a closed window's PDA is never created again (`open_window` only creates index `window_count`, which only grows).
- *Refunds keep the Window.* `refund_entry` needs the escrow and the window's signature, so a Refunding window stays open until every stake is back; refunded entries close afterwards.

**Entries.** An Entry is the PDA `["entry", window, owner]`: one entry per wallet per window, a second `enter` from the same wallet fails with `AlreadyEntered` (so the per-entry cap is also a per-wallet cap; several wallets remain possible, and the law gives a split entry the same expectation as a single one). The entry's index is assigned by `enter` from the window counter, in arrival order; `tally_page` / `settle_page` check every remaining account's owner, discriminator, window, index and PDA, so a page cannot skip, repeat or substitute an entry. `enter` creates the account itself (instead of Anchor's `init`, to return `AlreadyEntered` rather than a system error) with the same behaviour as `init`: an address someone pre-funded with lamports is topped up, allocated and assigned, so pre-funding a wallet's future Entry address cannot block it (`tests/cu-bench` pre-funds one before it enters).

**One entry, no draw.** A window that closes with exactly one entry goes from `seal` straight to `Drawn`, with an all-zero seed and no ORAO request. Its weight is the whole weight sum (`a * (a * (ROLL_MAX + r) - a * r) = ROLL_MAX * a^2 = S`), so its share is the whole curve buy whatever the roll; the fees are the same. Nothing is random, so nothing can be grinded or withheld, and there is no refund path (nothing to wait for). Windows with two or more entries always go through ORAO.

`create_market` also writes the launched token's Metaplex metadata (name, symbol, URI) through a hand-rolled CPI to Token Metadata `CreateMetadataAccountV3`, with `is_mutable = false`: nobody, the program included, can change it afterwards. The metadata PDA and the Metaplex program id are checked by address.

## 2. Update authority

**Current status: not deployed on mainnet-beta** (devnet: the v22 build, 2026-10-28).

**Decision (owner, 2026-10-28): the program launches upgradeable.** `scripts/mainnet-up.ps1` deploys it with the mainnet deployer key (`%USERPROFILE%\.config\ladle\ladle-mainnet-deployer.json`) as its upgrade authority and checks that on chain after the deploy. The reason is cost: while an upgrade authority exists, `solana program close` returns the ~2.0 SOL of program rent; after `--final` it never comes back.

What that means, without softening:

- **The holder of the upgrade authority can replace the program at any moment, with no delay and no notice.** A replaced program can change the roll law, the fee and every rule in §1, and can move everything the program's accounts hold: the stakes in the open rounds' escrows, the curve's SOL in the market's quote vault, the unsold LADLE in the token vault. Every "does not exist" in §1 holds only for the code as deployed, for as long as it is not replaced.
- **Stopping the game and closing the program.** `solana program close` ends the program for good: everything still in its accounts could never be moved again. So the owner stops the game first, and players are paid out automatically (`scripts/mainnet-shutdown.ps1`, `programs/ladle/src/instructions/wind_down.rs`):
  1. the program is upgraded to the **wind-down build** (`--features wind-down`): no new market, round or entry; rounds in flight finish (draw, or refund after the VRF timeout); sell back, refunds and closes work as before;
  2. `start_wind_down` starts each market's **notice: 7 days** of the cluster clock (`WIND_DOWN_NOTICE_SECS`), shown with its end date on every page of the site. Players exit by themselves meanwhile;
  3. after the notice, `force_exit(entry)` (anyone; the keeper's `--shutdown` sends it for every entry left) pays what is left in an entry **to its owner and nobody else**: the unsold share sold to the curve at that moment (exactly what `sell_back` would pay then), or the stake of a round that was refunded, **as SOL straight to the owner's wallet** (every market is quoted in wSOL, §5), with the entry's rent. The owner has nothing to do and no account to open, and nothing a player does with their token accounts can block it. Then every round closes (rent to its opener) and `close_market` closes the market once no round account is left and no LADLE is out (rents and the curve's rounding to its creator);
  4. `report.ts` reads every market, round and entry of the program (`create_market` is open to anyone: other markets' players are paid out the same way) and says `programClosable` only when no Entry and no Window account is left and the wind-down build is live; only then is the program closed. The keeper's `--shutdown` works program-wide: it serves every market of the program, given or not, and closes the entries a market closed by someone else left behind (`close_market` is permissionless and does not count entries; what such an entry holds is only its owner's rent, which `close_entry` returns without the market).

  Invariants of the wind-down build, checked on the real builds by `tests/cu-bench` (`pnpm bench:wind-down`): `force_exit` refuses before the notice ends and does not exist in the normal build; it pays only `entry.owner` (`has_one`; the payout account is fixed by the entry: the PDA ["unwrap", entry], closed into the entry, which closes to the owner), exactly the share's value or the stake, once (the entry closes in the same instruction); its caller only lends the unwrap account's rent and gets it back; a round opening under a normal build clears the notice, so a called-off shutdown needs a new, full one; after a full drain no program account, stake, share or lamport of a player is left; a player who handed their wSOL account to someone else (classic SPL Token lets an owner do that) is still paid out; an empty entry left open when the market closed still closes afterwards (final review, 2026-10-29). **What this does not change:** until the shutdown, and during it, the upgrade authority can still replace the program with anything (the paragraph above): the notice protects players from forgetting, not from the key holder. A market made before v25 cannot be closed (its rounds were not counted; its entries are still paid out), and LADLE taken to a wallet by a pre-v25 `claim` (devnet only) keeps its market open: closing the program then leaves that market's accounts (its creator's rent, and the SOL backing that LADLE, which its holders had 7 days to sell).
- **Upgrades and live accounts**: Anchor reads an account from its start, so a struct that grows breaks every account made before. `Market` ends with reserved bytes for that (a field is carved out of them: v25 took 17 of its 64 for the shutdown, 47 are left), and so does `Window` since the third review (64 bytes: the active window and a Refunding window outlive a round, and an unreadable one would stop the market's next round or freeze its refunds); `Entry` has none (its rent is the player's): a change to it must ship with a decoder for the old size in the same upgrade, or every live entry stops being sellable until it does (`programs/ladle/src/state.rs`, sizes pinned by a test).
- **Where the authority lives**: the deployer key file on the owner's PC until `scripts/mainnet-up.ps1 -UpgradeAuthority usb://ledger` moves it to a hardware wallet (§7).
- The draw's honesty therefore depends on the operator while this authority exists. The site must say so wherever it describes the program's guarantees (`docs/DEPLOY.md`, "Before mainnet": the security page and FAQ still describe the plan below and a program that is not deployed).

The two configurations that remove that trust, still the recommended path:

1. **Revoked after audit** (`solana program set-upgrade-authority --final`). A program whose law can change is a program whose draw can be biased by its operator; revocation is the only configuration where the honesty of the draw does not depend on us. It also gives up the program rent.
2. **72-hour minimum timelock** with an on-chain event on every proposed upgrade, via a timelock program holding the upgrade authority. Acceptable only as an interim stage before (1).

Until one of the two is in place, the site must not describe the draw as independent of the operator.

## 3. Randomness: where the trust is, and the attack surface

Source: **ORAO VRF** (`VRFzZoJdhFWL8rkvu87LpKM3RbcVezpMEc6X5GVDr7y`). Switchboard On-Demand was evaluated and excluded: its randomness implementations were deprecated by Switchboard in September 2026 (see `research/G2.md`). Measured latency, cost and the full attack analysis are in `research/G2.md`.

Flow (windows with 2 or more entries; 0 entries settle at `seal`, 1 entry is `Drawn` at `seal` without randomness, see §1): `seal` (only at or after `close_slot`) fixes the seal seed `sha256("ladle:vrf:v1" ‖ window ‖ close_slot ‖ entry_count ‖ entries_commitment)`, where `entries_commitment` is folded on every entry: it binds the final entry set. `request_vrf(salt)` (only in `Sealed`, once) asks ORAO for `sha256("ladle:vrf:req" ‖ seal seed ‖ salt)` and stores it as `vrf_seed`; the salt and the payer are in the `VrfRequested` event, so anyone can recompute the chain. ORAO's fulfilment authorities each sign the seed (Ed25519, deterministic) and ORAO XORs the signatures; the result is the 64-byte `seed` of the window. Every roll is `sha256(seed ‖ owner ‖ index)` — recomputable by anyone.

Why a salt (review 2026-10-28): the seal seed is computable by anyone from the Open window's fields, and ORAO accepts each seed once, from anyone (`SeedAlreadyInUse`). Requested at ORAO directly by a stranger, it could never be drawn for the window: every round with 2+ entries could be forced into a refund for ~0.002 SOL (`tests/cu-bench` `vrf_salt` reproduces it and checks the fix). The salt is chosen in the request itself; a stranger who copies the salt of a request in flight and lands first only makes that attempt fail, and the keeper retries with a new salt. Never replace the salt by letting `request_vrf` adopt a request someone already made at ORAO for the seal seed: anyone could then request the seal seed of a guessed final entry set before the close, read ORAO's answer (a few seconds), and add an entry to re-roll a bad one, with no collusion at all.

What `request_vrf` hands ORAO: the payer's signature, in a CPI to ORAO's upgradeable program with a fee read from ORAO's mutable network state. Since the second review the program measures what the payer lost across the CPI and fails above `MAX_VRF_REQUEST_COST_LAMPORTS` (0.01 SOL; today 0.00496 SOL is advanced, 0.00311 of it returned at fulfilment): a raised fee or a changed ORAO program cannot take the keeper's float.

| attacker | what they control | why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **participant** | when and how much to enter (one entry per wallet), how many wallets, their own pubkey (grinding) | the seed does not exist before `close_slot`, and `entries_commitment` includes every entry, so grinding a pubkey changes the seed for everyone, unpredictably; entries after close are rejected; there is no cancel |
| **window creator / market creator** | market parameters at creation, nothing afterwards | no parameter touches the seed; the creator cannot enter after close, cannot request before seal, cannot refund before timeout |
| **keeper / request payer** | when to call `seal`, `request_vrf`, `fulfill`, `tally_page`, `settle_page`, `refund_window`; the salt of its request | all are permissionless, so withholding only delays until someone else calls; the value cannot be dodged (`MustFulfill`); the keeper cannot re-request (one request per window, `VrfAlreadyRequested`); the salt does not choose the draw: ORAO's output for a new seed is unknown until ORAO answers on chain, and the first request that lands fixes the seed. The bounty goes to the request payer's quote ATA (`window.vrf_payer`): racing the keeper to the final page earns nothing |
| **slot leader** | transaction ordering and inclusion within a slot, the slot hash | the program never reads slot hashes, block hashes, timestamps or any chain entropy (`tests/idl-authority.ts` greps for it); ordering entries changes `entries_commitment` but the leader cannot predict ORAO's signatures over the resulting seed |
| **ORAO fulfilment authorities** | their signing keys | **this is the residual trust.** Signatures are deterministic, so a colluding quorum (3 of 3 on mainnet, all ORAO's) could compute the outcome as soon as the requested seed is known, never before the last entry has landed (the seal seed binds the entries), and they cannot enter after close. They could, in collusion with an entrant, *withhold* fulfilment on a bad outcome and force a timeout refund. Choosing among outcomes was possible before the salt too, by a different route: the requested seed was then the seal seed, fixed by the entries, so a quorum colluding with the **last entrant** could evaluate candidate entries (key, amount) offline and enter the one it liked at `close_slot - 1`. Since the salt (2026-10-28) the seed depends on the first `request_vrf`, so the colluding party must be **whoever sends that request** (an entrant alone no longer can): the salt narrows who can collude, it does not remove the collusion risk. The keeper sends the request right after the seal, in the sealing transaction; a third party has to beat it to that. We accept this trust (it is the trust already placed in ORAO's keys) because the alternative left every round open to a forced refund by anyone for ~0.002 SOL, and every on-chain alternative is worse (leader-influenceable). |

Assumption to **verify on devnet** before mainnet: ORAO `fulfill_v2` keeps the request account (reallocated to the fulfilled layout) and does not close it. The program treats a missing request account as a hard error on the refund path for this reason.

## 4. Accounting invariants

Proven at the base unit by `tests/props/run.ts` on 10,000 adversarial windows (reference TypeScript law, line-by-line twin of `programs/ladle/src/law.rs`, cross-checked by `tests/vectors/law-vectors.json`):

- `Σ shares == tokens_out` for every window; each share within 1 unit of its exact value; remainder spread by cumulative quota rounding, never dropped, never dumped on the last entry.
- `Σ stakes == to_curve + platform + bounty + host` on settlement; `Σ stakes == Σ refunds` on refund. When a market's integration host has no quote account at the final page, `host == 0` and its share stays in `to_curve` (final review: a host could otherwise stop its market's settlement queue by closing an account).
- Curve rounding is always against the trader; selling back an entire window returns at most what the curve received.
- Settlement is paginated with a strict cursor: no entry skipped or double-counted, replays are no-ops. `settle_page` carries `floor(tokens_out * cum / S)` from one entry to the next instead of recomputing it (one 256-bit division per entry); `programs/ladle/src/tests_pages.rs` checks it share for share against the two-division step on whole windows of 1, 2, 50, 100, 500 and 2048 entries, and `tests/cu-bench` checks `sum(shares) == tokens_out` and the fee split at the lamport on the SBF build for the same sizes.
- `roll_sum` and `tokens_out` are frozen at finalize; the curve executes once per window, in window order.

## 5. Token-2022 and the quote mint

`create_market` accepts only wrapped SOL as the quote mint (`QuoteMintNotWsol`, final review 2026-10-29). Until then any classic SPL Token mint without a freeze authority was accepted, and the shutdown paid such a market's entries into their owners' token accounts. The final review showed two ways that let one player hold the program close hostage: a classic SPL Token account's owner can be handed to someone else for good (its associated address is then taken by a foreign owner, and the payout had nowhere to go), and every payout into a missing account made whoever drains pay that account's rent (farmable per wallet). SOL to the owner's wallet is the only payout nobody can block, and every planned market was wSOL anyway; the non-wSOL branches of `force_exit` and `close_market` are gone. Before the second review Token-2022 mints were accepted unless they carried `TransferFeeConfig`, `TransferHook` or `PermanentDelegate`; a mint with `Pausable` passed (its pause authority could stop every transfer: entries, the final payout, refunds, sell backs, the same power as a freeze authority), and on a market with an integration the host could add `MemoTransfer` to its own token account and make every round's final page fail. Token-2022 is now refused as a whole (the extension parsing went, -784 bytes net with the other fixes), and `refund_entry` pays only the owner's associated token account (a stranger can no longer refund into an account the player never sees).

## 6. Known limitations and what is NOT verified

- **One round that cannot finish holds every later one** (rounds settle in index order, and a `Drawn` round has no refund path). Since the second review `open_window` refuses a round while 8 wait to settle (`MAX_WINDOWS_IN_FLIGHT`, `TooManyWindowsPending`): the stakes at risk stay bounded to those rounds; the keeper raises `settlement_stalled` / `rounds_paused` with `/health` at 503 and refuses to start with a treasury the build does not have; the site shows the market as paused; `docs/DEPLOY.md` "A round that does not settle" says what to do, and a program upgrade is the last way out (it is why the program stays upgradeable). A market whose VRF timeout never ends cannot be created any more (`vrf_timeout_slots <= 9000`).
- **Devnet: the v22 build ran there (2026-10-28), not this one.** The SBF build (387,040 bytes, `opt-level = "z"`, SBPF v3, accepted by devnet and mainnet-beta) runs end to end on LiteSVM with the mainnet feature set (`tests/cu-bench`: open, 1 to 2048 enters, seal, `request_vrf` through a stand-in for ORAO's `request_v2` (`tests/cu-bench/mock-orao`: same accounts, zero-seed and seed-in-use refusals, request PDA check, fee, pending layout), fulfill from an ORAO-shaped account, tally/settle pages, close the window, then sell back and close every entry against the closed window, the refusals listed in §1, a refunded window, and the review's regressions: escrows created by a stranger before `open_window`, the seal seed and a copied salt used up at ORAO first, a bot's final page, a stake below the minimum, the slippage bound; and the shutdown on a normal and a wind-down build: the notice, every forced exit to the lamport, the market closed, nothing left); `cargo clippy --lib` is clean and the host tests pass. ORAO itself (its real request and fulfilment) is not exercised there. The Anchor suite in `tests/anchor` (same-slot bundles, a 50-wallet crowd, request-after-seal, no-cancel, refund on silent VRF, pagination over 500 entries, Token-2022 rejection, bias attempts from the four positions) is written and **has not been run**.
- **No audit.**
- Compute units are measured on LiteSVM (`docs/DEPLOY.md`): the heaviest page (`settle_page`, 48 entries) uses 549,556 of the 1.4M ceiling; the keeper sends pages of 20 / 28 entries (what fits a legacy transaction) with an explicit compute-unit limit. `request_vrf` is measured only against the stand-in for ORAO.
- The program carries no error sentences (`#[msg]`) and no "Instruction: X" log line, to keep the deploy rent down; logs name the error (`Error Code: AlreadyEntered`), clients map it to a sentence (`sdk/src/errors.ts`).
- The platform treasury is fixed at build time (`LADLE_TREASURY`, the devnet deployer when unset; `docs/DEPLOY.md` "Build"): a mainnet build without it would pay the devnet key. `scripts/mainnet-up.ps1` makes that hard to get wrong: `-Treasury` is mandatory, the devnet key is refused, and the built `.so` must contain the program id and the given treasury and must not contain the devnet key before anything is sent. Clients configured with another key fail on chain (`create_market` address check, `PlatformAccountMismatch`) and move nothing; `resolveTreasury` refuses to fall back to the devnet key on mainnet-beta. Changing the treasury later means an upgrade (§2).
- The program id is the keypair in `target/deploy/ladle-keypair.json` (deployed on devnet on 2026-10-28 with the v22 build, not on mainnet-beta; `scripts/mainnet-up.ps1` backs it up to `%USERPROFILE%\.config\ladle\`). Until the mainnet deploy, whoever has that file can deploy their own program at this address first; after it, the file has no power.
- **Single randomness provider.** Switchboard's randomness is deprecated (September 2026); ORAO is the only viable VRF on Solana today. If ORAO stops answering or refuses requests, every pending window refunds in full after 900 slots and no new window can be drawn: the keeper never lets a failing request hold the seal back (a failed seal bundle is retried as seal alone), retries `request_vrf` with a new salt, alerts after 5 failures in a row, and sends `refund_window` once the timeout passed, requested or not (`keeper/test/loop.test.ts`). It refunds the stakes of wallets that already have a quote token account; it never pays that account's rent for someone else (anyone could drain it that way), so the other owners take their stake back from the site (`PulledClient.refund`, paid by them). Measured ORAO latency (n=314, mainnet, 2026-10-16/17): median 4 s, p90 72 s, p99 113 s; windows are pipelined on a fixed slot grid and settle in order, so a slow oracle delays settlement, never entry or refund.
- A dead keeper delays: every step is permissionless, but the site only offers the refund. A round left `Open` past its close waits until someone seals it; a window stuck in `Sealed` with no request ever sent refunds only after the timeout counted from `sealed_slot`. To shut down, drain the keeper (`KEEPER_DRAIN=1`: no new round, everything finished, refunded and closed, then it exits) instead of stopping it mid-round.
- **Dust entries** cannot make the operator pay for draws: `enter` refuses stakes below `MIN_ENTRY_AMOUNT` (0.1 SOL), so a round that asks ORAO totals at least 0.2 SOL and its 1 % fee (0.002 SOL) covers the draw (~0.00186 SOL). The keeper's float itself receives only the 0.1 % bounty and needs refilling from the treasury; `KEEPER_VRF_PER_DAY` can cap its draws.
- **Escrow squatting** is harmless: every future window's escrow address is public and anyone can create it with the ATA program; `open_window` accepts an existing escrow (`init_if_needed`: mint, owner = the window, token program and address checked; only the window can set a delegate or a close authority), and `close_window` sweeps whatever a stranger put there into the quote vault. Before the 2026-10-28 review one such account (~0.002 SOL) stopped the market for good. The market's own vaults are created with the market in `create_market`, whose address depends on a fresh mint key: a squatted vault only makes that creation fail, and a retry with a new mint goes through.
- Empty windows and refunded windows must be processed in settlement order; a window waiting behind a pending one is delayed, not lost.

## 7. Keys and what an attacker could do with each

Everything the program does after `create_market` is permissionless except the user-signed money paths (§1): no key in this list is an authority inside the program, except the upgrade authority, which sits above it (§2).

| key / secret | where it lives | an attacker with it could | could not | keep it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **upgrade authority** = mainnet deployer (`ladle-mainnet-deployer.json`) until moved | the owner's PC, `%USERPROFILE%\.config\ladle\` (plain JSON file) | replace the program and take every escrow, the curve's SOL and the unsold LADLE (§2); close the program without the shutdown and lock all of it; spend the SOL left on the deployer | nothing is beyond it | moved to a hardware wallet right after the deploy: `scripts/mainnet-up.ps1 -UpgradeAuthority usb://ledger` (typed `HANDOVER`; the Ledger must sign, so a typo cannot lock the program; checked with `solana program show`). Without it the script asks for a typed `HOTKEY`. No recovery words are printed (the console can be read back): the key file is backed up offline (typed `BACKEDUP`). It cannot be born on the Ledger: the CLI would make the Ledger sign every write transaction of the deploy |
| **treasury** (`-Treasury`, the owner's wallet) | the owner's wallet (hardware wallet or multisig advised) | take the platform fees and the 0.05 SOL market fee already paid to it | touch any player's funds, the program or a round | a hardware wallet. It is compiled in for good: `mainnet-up.ps1` makes the owner retype its last 8 characters from the wallet and refuses a program-owned address (a Squads multisig account instead of its vault); on devnet, the 0.05 SOL creation fee arriving in the owner's wallet proves the key is theirs |
| **keeper** (`ladle-mainnet-keeper.json`, `/etc/ladle/keeper.json` on the VPS, `0640 root:ladle-keeper`) | the VPS (hot: it signs every round), readable by the keeper's own user only: the chat relay, which faces the internet, runs as another user and its unit hides the keeper's files | spend its float (0.5 SOL) and the bounty it holds; stop running rounds (the open one waits unsealed; a sealed one refunds after the VRF timeout) | redirect a stake, a share, a fee or a refund (the program fixes every recipient), see the seed early, cancel an entry | its own key, never `id.json`; only the float it needs; the low-balance alert |
| **program keypair** (`target/deploy/ladle-keypair.json` + backup) | the owner's PC | before the mainnet deploy: deploy another program at this address first | anything once the program is deployed | private until the deploy |
| **server RPC key** (`keeper.env`, `0600 root`, read by systemd only) | the VPS | use the paid plan's quota (a bill, or a stalled keeper when credits run out) | sign anything | restricted to the VPS's IP; a spending cap; **on its own account or plan**, apart from the browser key |
| **browser RPC key** (`NEXT_PUBLIC_RPC_URL`) | public: every visitor's browser | use the quota from anywhere: an origin allowlist only stops other websites, a script sends any `Origin` header | sign anything | restricted to `https://useladle.fun`, a hard spending cap and a rate limit; on another account than the keeper's, so burning it cannot stall the rounds |
| **`CHAT_RESUME_SECRET`** (`chat.env`) | the VPS | forge chat resume tokens (HMAC), so post in the round chat as any wallet without its signature | anything on chain | random, 32 bytes, generated by the script; changing it signs everyone out |
| GitHub push to `main` / the Vercel account | the owner's accounts | publish a site that asks wallets to sign other transactions (the wallet still shows what it signs) | touch the program or its funds | 2FA on both |

The scripts: `scripts/mainnet-up.ps1` is run by the owner; it asks for the treasury's last 8 characters, then for a typed word before the deploy (`DEPLOY`, and `UNTESTED` for a build that differs from the devnet one), before the market (`CREATE`) and before moving the upgrade authority (`HANDOVER`); a failed RPC read stops it (it is never taken for "not deployed"). `scripts/mainnet-recover.ps1` sends nothing and only prints commands; `-Owner` is checked first (its last 8 characters retyped from the wallet, an existing wallet, and the printed transfers keep the CLI's refusal to send to an account that does not exist); it prints `solana program close` only once `report.ts` says `programClosable` (§2: after the shutdown, or with no market at all). `scripts/mainnet-shutdown.ps1` (the owner runs it) stops the game: typed `STOP`, `NOTICE`, `DRAIN` (when the VPS keeper does not), `CLOSE`; the upgrade goes through a buffer the deployer writes, so a Ledger signs one transaction. The mainnet keys are created with `solana-keygen new` in `%USERPROFILE%\.config\ladle\`, silently (no recovery words on the console), never in the repository. No log line or alert of the keeper carries an RPC URL whole: errors are cut to the host (web3.js quotes the URL, key included, in its fetch errors).
