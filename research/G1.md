# G1 — Prior-art gate: "random fill size at a fixed price with a conserved total"

**Product under review:** PULLED (Solana).
**Date of research:** 2026-10-23.
**Researcher:** Claude (automated web research; WebSearch + WebFetch only, no on-chain inspection).

## 0. The claim being tested

PULLED mechanic, as given to the researcher:

1. Everyone pays into a fixed-price window (e.g. 20 seconds). Every participant pays the same unit price.
2. When the window closes, the program executes **one** bonding-curve buy for the whole window.
3. A **single external random seed** then decides how the tokens that the curve sold are **split** among the window's participants. Each participant's **fill size is random**.
4. **Conservation:** the sum of all fills equals exactly what the curve sold. The house holds no inventory and takes none of the variance; variance is purely redistributed among the participants of the same window.
5. Each participant can immediately sell back to the curve.

**Gate question:** does a LIVE product already exist where the fill/allocation size is drawn at random, at a fixed price, with a conserved total among the buyers of the same batch?

A product counts as **SAME** only if all four of these hold: (a) fixed price per unit for everyone in the batch, (b) the *quantity* each buyer receives is random (not merely *whether* they receive a fixed quantity), (c) the random quantities of the batch sum to a fixed, pre-determined total (zero-sum among buyers), (d) the house/operator holds no inventory and does not absorb the variance.

## 3. Analysis

### 3.1 What is closest, and why it still differs

Ranked by structural proximity to PULLED:

1. **Pooled-pot jackpots (Solpot-type).** Share with PULLED: a fixed-duration window, everyone pays in, a random outcome decides how a **conserved** pot is divided, the operator holds no inventory and only takes a rake. Differ: the split is winner-take-all (one participant gets ~100 %, the rest 0 %) rather than a random *fill* for everyone; nothing is purchased from a bonding curve; there is no unit price and no asset to sell back. It is a casino primitive, not an allocation primitive.

2. **Red-packet random split (hongbao).** Share with PULLED: the exact arithmetic — a fixed total divided into N random positive parts that sum exactly to the total. Differ: it is a gift from one sender; nobody pays a fixed price; there is no curve, no sell-back, and no on-chain Solana product was found. This is prior art for the *algorithm* (bounded sequential draws, stick-breaking/Dirichlet), not for the *product*.

3. **IDO lotteries (Polkastarter, DAO Maker, Binance Launchpad, Nigerian IPO ballots).** Share: fixed price, randomness, fixed total supply. Differ: the random variable is **binary per participant** (win a fixed-size slot or nothing). The *quantity* a winner receives is fixed in advance; the total is conserved only because the slot count is fixed. PULLED's random variable is the continuous fill size of every participant, none of whom gets zero by construction (unless the design allows zero fills).

4. **Gacha packs (Collector Crypt and its frontends: Jupiter Gacha, Solflare Packs, Rarible Gacha Station; Beezie; Phygitals; the Oct 2026 pump.fun integration).** Share: fixed price, random outcome, instant sell-back. Differ on the two criteria that matter most: every pull returns **exactly one unit** (a card token) whose *value* is random, and the **house owns the inventory and bears the variance** (vault, buyback book, published odds, EV claims). Nothing is pooled or conserved among the buyers of a batch; Collector Crypt's own VRF doc stresses per-signature independence, with other buyers affecting only inventory depletion. Pump.fun's integration is a Collector Crypt tab inside the app and leaves pump.fun's deterministic curve fills untouched.

5. **Magic Eden Lucky Buy.** Share: pay for a random outcome, VRF, cash-out option. Differ: the price is chosen by the buyer via a probability slider (not one fixed price for all), the outcome is binary, and the counterparty is the seller/marketplace, not the other buyers.

6. **Deterministic launchpads (Daos.fun, Fjord, Legion, Echo/Sonar, Metaplex Genesis, CoinList, Meteora, LaunchLab/StonkFun).** Share: fixed price (where applicable) and sometimes a conserved pro-rata total. Differ: zero randomness in fill size.

### 3.2 Why Solpad's "random-ten" is NOT a random fill size with a conserved sum

The brief asked for this in writing. Based on the only available description (README of `h01027157268-bot/solpad`; the official solpad.io domains served no app on 2026-10-23, so the live product could not be checked):

- **The fill is deterministic.** Every buy is priced by the pump.fun-style curve formula `tokens_out = v_tokens * sol_in / (v_sol + sol_in)`. Two buyers paying the same SOL at the same curve state receive the same tokens. There is no random variable in the quantity delivered to a buyer.
- **The randomness selects recipients, not amounts.** "6% drawn by ten random holders" means the program draws *which ten wallets* receive a bonus; the size of the bonus pool (6 % of the trade tax) is fixed by rule, and the README does not describe the ten winners' shares as random (it reads as a split of a fixed pot among a fixed number of winners).
- **The pot is a tax, not the purchase.** The 1/3/6 split applies to a **10 % tax levied on top of every trade**. The conserved quantity is the tax pool, which is money *extracted* from buyers and sellers and routed to the platform, top holders and random holders. PULLED's conserved quantity is the **tokens the curve actually sold to the window**; no extra levy is created, and the platform does not receive a share of what is redistributed.
- **Non-winners lose nothing from their fill; winners gain from a side-pot.** In Solpad's design the outcome for a buyer is "my deterministic fill, plus maybe a bonus later". In PULLED the outcome is "a random fraction of the window's purchase, where my gain is exactly someone else's shortfall in the same window". The former is a positive-sum bonus funded by a fee; the latter is zero-sum redistribution among the batch.
- **No window batching.** Solpad's draws are tied to holder sets and trade tax over time, not to a fixed-price window closed by a single seed with one aggregate curve buy.
- **Not live.** The README states the lottery path is mid-migration with compile errors, no audit, and mainnet deployment pending. Even if it were SAME, it would not satisfy the gate's "live" requirement.

Conclusion for Solpad: **DIFFERENT** (random *who* for a fee bonus, deterministic *how much* for the purchase).

## 4. Conclusion

**PASS.**

As of 2026-10-23, no live product was found that draws each participant's **fill size** at random, at a **fixed price** shared by the whole window, with the fills **summing exactly to what the curve sold** and the house holding no inventory and taking no variance.

Everything found falls into one of five non-matching classes:

1. Deterministic launchpads (pro-rata, FCFS, merit, auction): no randomness.
2. Allocation lotteries (Polkastarter, DAO Maker, Binance, Legion-style selection): random *who*, fixed *how much*.
3. Gacha / packs / blind boxes (Collector Crypt and all its frontends incl. pump.fun's Oct 2026 tab, Beezie, Phygitals, Dropzone, Candy Machine, Lucky Buy): fixed price, one unit per pull, **house inventory and house variance**.
4. Fee-funded holder lotteries (Solpad "random-ten" as described on GitHub, Solotto, Liquid Bank, DragonSol, Pump.lotto): randomness on a tax side-pot; purchase fills deterministic.
5. Conserved random pots outside token sales (Solpot-type jackpots, red-packet splits): the arithmetic exists but no price, no curve, no asset fill.

The nearest structural neighbours are (a) pooled 60-second jackpots and (b) red-packet random splitting; neither sells an asset at a fixed price with a per-participant random fill. No academic or protocol design combining these elements was found either.
