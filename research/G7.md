# G7 — Does the loop actually turn?

Date: 2026-10-23. Author: research agent. Status: **UNVERIFIED** (see Conclusion). Part (b), our own re-entry measurement, is **NOT DONE**.

Question: the PULLED thesis assumes a "rip → bad draw → buy back / sell back → rip again" loop with its own engine. Part (a) checks the public evidence from Collector Crypt and its distribution partners (Jupiter Gacha, Solflare Packs, Pump.fun). Part (b) specifies how we will measure re-entry after a bad draw on our own testnet.

Rule applied throughout: no number is restated without a source; anything not retrievable is labelled UNVERIFIED. "Verified" means we read the figure in the named source on the date given; it does not mean the source's methodology was audited.

## 3. Analysis

### 3.1 Is the "20.5×" reproducible?

**No.** The figure is a single undefined analyst number quoted in a newsletter (2026-05-12), with no method, no window and no other source. We could not find it in the Q2 token-holder report excerpts, Pine, Messari, Bitquery or Four Pillars.

What *can* be said (our own inference, labelled as such): the platform returns ≈ 91–94 % of pack spend as instant buybacks (Bitquery 94.2 %, DefiLlama 91.85 %, Pine 94.1 %), and ≈ 68–72 % of wallets/volume auto-sell back immediately. If every buyback dollar were re-ripped, gross volume per fresh dollar would be `1 / (1 − RTP)` ≈ 12–17×. A 20.5× figure therefore requires either a higher RTP in the period Doshi measured (Messari reported July buyback ratios of 95.6–99.1 % by rarity tier, which would give 23–100×) or a different denominator (e.g. physical inventory value, which the newsletter author assumes). It is **plausible as "gross volume per $ of inventory or net deposit", not as "each card is pulled 20.5 times"**. Treat as UNVERIFIED and do not quote it in product material.

### 3.2 What share loses?

The best dataset (Bitquery, 17,544 wallets, 7 months, realised cash) says **≈ 78 % of wallets lose, median −$50, and the loss is concentrated in the whales who supply ≈ 88 % of the money** (−$41 k average for the 714 wallets above $100 k). The house edge per rip is small (≈ 6 % gross margin, 85–93 % buyback) and consistent for four quarters (Pine). The loop's economics are therefore "slow bleed at high frequency", not "big loss per rip" — the typical loser loses little per event, which is exactly the regime in which re-entry is most likely, but nobody has measured it.

### 3.3 What is known about re-play after losses?

- **Nothing conditional on losing.** Every retention figure found is unconditional (bought again in a later month: 34 % Jupiter cohort, 48 % site cohort, 58 % of Q2 $1k-pack buyers returned in Q3).
- **The behavioural proxy is strong but ambiguous:** ≈ 68–72 % immediate auto-sellback, "YOLO mode" auto-re-rip up to 20 packs, largest wallet cycling $37.7 M while keeping 0.5 %. This shows the *money* loops, but mostly inside a few hundred wallets, and it does not distinguish "re-enter after a bad draw" from "programmatic churning regardless of outcome".
- **Aggregate trend is not a self-sustaining engine:** pack sales fell 37 % from June to September (212.2 → 134.5 M) while active wallets rose (7,051 → 9,127 thirty-day actives) and spend per active wallet fell from $9,300 to $4,800. Each step-up (Jupiter 13 Jul, Solflare June, Pump.fun 19 Oct) came from a **new distribution channel**, and the Jupiter channel decayed from 35 % to 6–9 % of sales within weeks. The record day of ≈ 20 Oct is a distribution event (Pump.fun), not evidence of retention.
- **Interpretation for PULLED:** the Collector Crypt data shows a loop that *turns* in volume terms because (a) buyback cash is instantly available to re-spend, (b) a small number of whales churn programmatically, and (c) partners keep injecting new wallets. It does **not** show that a losing *retail* player comes back. Our design cannot assume that engine exists until we measure it.

## 4. Protocol for our own measurement (NOT DONE)

> **Status: NOT DONE. No testnet cohort has been run. The numbers below are targets and definitions, not results.**

### 4.1 Objective

Estimate **R_bad** = share of participants who re-enter (place a new entry) within a fixed window after a *bad draw*, with a 95 % confidence interval, on our testnet program, and compare it with **R_good** (re-entry after a non-bad draw) and with **R_agent** (the same metric for scripted agents, as a floor/ceiling control).

### 4.2 Events to index (from program logs / Anchor events, by slot)

| Event | Fields |
|---|---|
| `WindowOpened` | `window_id, open_slot, window_slots, pot_mint` |
| `Entered` | `window_id, wallet, amount, entry_slot, tx_sig` |
| `WindowClosed` | `window_id, close_slot, total_pot, n_entries, n_wallets` |
| `VrfRequested` / `VrfFulfilled` | `window_id, request_slot, fulfil_slot` (also feeds G2) |
| `Drawn` | `window_id, wallet, prorata_share, payout, payout_slot` — one per wallet per window |
| `Claimed` / `SoldBack` (if a buyback exists) | `window_id, wallet, amount, slot` |

Indexer: subscribe to program logs (websocket) **and** backfill with `getSignaturesForAddress` + `getTransaction` so no event is lost; store in SQLite/Postgres keyed by `(window_id, wallet)`. Record slots, not timestamps; convert with G6's 216.5 ms only for reporting. Handle Alpenglow's `bank_id` field if consuming block streams (G6 §4).

### 4.3 Definitions

- **Prorata** for wallet *w* in window *k*: `prorata_wk = total_pot_k × amount_wk / Σ amount_k`.
- **Share ratio**: `s_wk = payout_wk / prorata_wk`.
- **Bad draw**: `s_wk < 1` (strictly below prorata). Secondary buckets for sensitivity: *very bad* `s < 0.5`, *wipe-out* `payout = 0`, *good* `s ≥ 1`, *jackpot* `s ≥ 3`.
- **Re-entry**: the same wallet emits an `Entered` in **any** later window whose `entry_slot` is within **W = 1,660 slots (≈ 6 min at 216.5 ms)** after `payout_slot`; secondary windows **W₂ = 16,600 slots (≈ 1 h)** and **W₃ = 400,000 slots (≈ 24 h)**. The short window is the "engine" test (does the loss itself pull the player back); the long windows catch habit.
- **Re-entry amount ratio**: `amount_next / amount_prev` (are losers doubling down? stabilising? tapering?).
- **Unit of analysis**: a *draw* (one `(window, wallet)` payout). A wallet can contribute several draws; report both draw-level and wallet-level (first bad draw per wallet) rates, and cluster the CI by wallet.

### 4.4 Sample size

Target **1,000 bad draws** (not 1,000 draws). At p ≈ 0.3 that gives a 95 % CI half-width of ≈ 2.8 pts (binomial; wider after wallet clustering). With `window_slots = 92` (≈ 20 s) and ≈ 60 % of draws being bad under a prorata-centred payout distribution, 1,000 bad draws need ≈ 1,700 draws — with 10 wallets per window that is ≈ 170 windows ≈ **1 hour of continuous windows**; with real users at 2–3 per window it is ≈ 600 windows ≈ **3.5 hours of play**, or several sessions. Pre-register the stopping rule (stop at 1,000 bad draws, not at a nice-looking result).

### 4.5 How to run it

**Phase A — agents (controls, can run today on devnet/localnet):**
1. `always-reenter` bots → should measure R ≈ 1 (validates the indexer and the window definition).
2. `never-reenter` bots → R ≈ 0 (validates no false positives from clock skew / slot misattribution).
3. `budget-martingale` bots (re-enter after a loss until budget exhausted) and `loss-averse` bots (re-enter only after a win) → confirms the pipeline separates R_bad from R_good.
Agents measure the **instrument**, not the hypothesis. Report R_agent separately and never pool with humans.

**Phase B — real users (the actual gate):**
1. Testnet with faucet-funded play money, but a **scarce** budget per wallet (e.g. 20 entries) so that re-entry carries an opportunity cost; unlimited faucets make R meaningless.
2. Recruit ≥ 150 distinct wallets (Discord/X of the project, no paid incentives tied to number of entries; a flat completion reward only). Record wallet creation time to flag sybils; dedupe by funding source.
3. Run windows back-to-back for the session; UI shows seconds via the G6 conversion.
4. Export the event table and compute R_bad, R_good, R_very_bad, amount ratios, and time-to-re-entry distribution (in slots).
5. Pre-registered analysis script committed before the session starts.

### 4.6 Thresholds (pre-registered)

| Result (short window W, real users, wallet-clustered CI) | Reading |
|---|---|
| R_bad ≥ 0.50 **and** R_bad ≥ R_good − 0.05 | Loop has an engine: a bad draw does not deter re-entry. **PASS** |
| 0.30 ≤ R_bad < 0.50 | Weak engine; the product needs an external driver (distribution, rewards). **UNVERIFIED → iterate** |
| R_bad < 0.30 **or** R_bad < R_good − 0.20 | **No engine**: bad draws end sessions. **FAIL** — redesign payout curve/UX before mainnet |
| Any result where R_agent controls fail (always ≠ ~1, never ≠ ~0) | Instrument invalid; redo |

Also report, without threshold: median re-entry latency in slots; share of re-entries that increase the stake; R by stake tier.

### 4.7 Known limits

Testnet money is not real money; expect R_bad to be **over-estimated** vs mainnet. Collector Crypt's data suggests real loops are whale-driven; our Phase B should therefore also report R_bad for the top-decile spenders separately. Results from Phase B are a lower bound on *design* viability, not a forecast of mainnet revenue.

## 5. Conclusion

**UNVERIFIED.**

- **Not FAIL:** the public data confirm that money does recycle at Collector Crypt — ≈ 91–94 % of pack spend comes straight back as buyback cash, ≈ 68–72 % of wallets/volume auto-sell immediately, Q3 pack sales were a record $433.5 M, and distribution partners produce step-changes ($9 M/5 days Jupiter, ≈ $2 M day-one Pump.fun, record ≈ $22–24 M day on ≈ 20 Oct 2026).
- **Not PASS:** (1) the "20.5 buybacks per card" figure is a single undefined quote and not reproducible; (2) no source measures re-entry after a *losing* draw — every retention number is unconditional; (3) the loop is carried by a few hundred wallets (548 wallets = 83 % of Q3 spend; 78 % of wallets lose, median −$50) and by successive new distribution channels, while organic pack sales fell 37 % from June to September; (4) several headline numbers given to us were imprecise (Solflare "$5 M" = insured value, Jupiter "92 % new" = new to Collector Crypt, "55 %" = share not decline, StonkFun +250 % = token price unrelated to gacha).
- **Our own measurement (Section 4) is NOT DONE.** Until Phase B produces R_bad with a CI, PULLED must not assume the loop has an engine; the product plan should treat distribution, not retention-after-loss, as the proven driver.
