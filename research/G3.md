# G3 — Is the expectation really fair?

**Date of measurement:** 2026-10-23 (results file: `research/data/G3-results.json`, run log: `research/data/G3-run.log`)
**Verdict: PASS for the linear law we ship. FAIL for the multiplicative law in the brief, which was rejected and redesigned.**

## 1. The question

The brief's first draft of the law was multiplicative:

```
share_i ∝ amount_i × roll_i / Σ(amount_j × roll_j)        (MULT)
```

The expectation of a ratio is not the ratio of expectations, so nothing guarantees that a small entry and a large entry have the same expected share relative to their pro-rata. The tolerance set by the brief: **no stake decile may be systematically advantaged or disadvantaged by more than 0.5 %**.

## 2. Method

Monte Carlo, float64, fixed PRNG (sfc32, seed `0x5eed0000 + 7919·len(key)` per family), reproducible bit for bit with:

```
node --experimental-strip-types sim/src/g3.ts 2000000 3
```

- **10,000,000 windows** in total: 2,000,000 per composition family, five families:
  - `two` — 2 players, log-normal stakes
  - `fifty` — 50 players, log-normal stakes (σ = 1)
  - `whale` — 1 entry of 10 units vs 10 entries of 1 unit
  - `power` — power-law stakes (Pareto α = 1.5), 2 to 200 players (202,042,797 entries)
  - `equal` — 10 equal stakes (symmetry control)
- Roll law: `r = floor((ROLL_MAX+1) · u³)`, `u` uniform on [0,1), `ROLL_MAX = 10 000` (the law shipped; a `k = 1` uniform variant was also run, see §6).
- For every entry: `ratio = realised share / pro-rata share`, binned by **decile of relative stake** `a_i / A` (decile edges computed on a 100,000-window calibration pass per family; families with fewer than 10 distinct stake classes are binned by class).
- Reported: mean ratio per decile and its 95 % confidence interval. A law **fails** if, for any decile, `|mean − 1| − CI₉₅ > 0.5 %` (i.e. the whole interval lies beyond the tolerance).
- The integer-exact rounding is **not** what this gate studies; it is proven separately by the property test (G5).

## 3. Results — multiplicative law (MULT): FAIL

| family | worst decile deviation | direction |
|---|---|---|
| two | **+265.5 %** (smallest decile: ratio 3.66 ± 0.02); largest decile 0.81 | small entries favoured, large entries robbed |
| fifty | +6.9 % (smallest) / −3.7 % (largest) | same |
| whale | small entries **+27.4 %** (1.274 ± 0.001), the whale **−27.4 %** (0.726 ± 0.001) | same |
| power | +17.9 % (smallest decile) | same |
| equal | 0.0 % | symmetric by construction |

Under MULT the expected multiplier of a stake is a decreasing function of its relative size. Two players, one at 10 % and one at 90 %: the small one expects ~3.7× its pro-rata, the big one ~0.7×. The law is not "unfair at the margin": it is a transfer from large to small stakes hidden behind randomness. It also has a hole: when every roll in the window is 0 (0.2 % of two-player windows under `k = 3`), `Σ a_j r_j = 0` and the window cannot be settled at all (4,209 such windows observed in the `two` family).

**Rejected.**

## 4. Redesign — linear law (LINEAR): PASS

```
share_i = tokens × (a_i / A) × (1 + (r_i − r̄) / ROLL_MAX),     r̄ = Σ a_j r_j / A
```

The multiplier is **linear** in the entry's own roll. Linearity makes expectation pass through: `E[r_i] = E[r̄]`, so `E[multiplier_i] = 1` for every entry regardless of size — analytically, not approximately. `Σ share_i = tokens` by algebra (the `r̄` term cancels when summed with weights `a_i / A`). The multiplier lies in `[0, 2]` because `|r_i − r̄| ≤ ROLL_MAX`. The weight sum `ROLL_MAX · A²` is strictly positive for any non-empty window, so a zero-weight window is impossible.

Measured (10,000,000 windows):

| family | worst decile deviation | all deciles within tolerance? |
|---|---|---|
| two | 0.106 % (CI ±0.115 %, includes 1) | yes |
| fifty | 0.012 % | yes |
| whale | 0.012 % (small: 1.00012 ± 0.00013, whale: 0.99988 ± 0.00021) | yes |
| power | 0.046 % | yes |
| equal | 0.000 % | yes |

Every decile's 95 % interval contains 1.000. Full per-decile tables with intervals are in `G3-results.json` and are displayed in the interface (`/odds`).

## 5. What a small player's multiplier actually looks like (LINEAR, k = 3)

Smallest-stake decile, from the same run:

| family | P(multiplier < 1) | p10 | median | p90 | p99 | p99.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fifty | 62.5 % | 0.725 | 0.885 | 1.475 | 1.745 | 1.825 |
| power | 62.1 % | 0.725 | 0.895 | 1.485 | 1.755 | 1.855 |
| whale (the 10 small) | 60.4 % | 0.645 | 0.935 | 1.475 | 1.775 | 1.855 |
| equal | 61.9 % | 0.725 | 0.915 | 1.435 | 1.695 | 1.785 |

Reading: about six entries in ten end **below** their pro-rata (typically around 0.9×), one in ten ends above ~1.45×, one in a hundred above ~1.75×. The maximum is below 2× by construction. This is the shape published in `/docs/ODDS.md` and on `/odds`. Note the honest consequence of exact fairness plus non-negativity: the upside is bounded at 2× pro-rata. A law with a longer right tail would either break fairness (MULT) or require negative shares.

## 6. Sensitivity to the roll shape

The same run with `k = 1` (uniform rolls, `G3-results-k1.json`): LINEAR passes identically (worst 0.067 %); MULT still fails (worst +66 % on `two`, +7.5 % on `whale`). The pass of the linear law does not depend on the roll distribution; the failure of the multiplicative law does not disappear with a tamer one.

## 7. Conclusion

- The law of the brief is **mathematically unfair** by 7 to 265 % depending on composition. Rejected.
- The shipped law is **exactly fair in expectation for every stake size**, measured within ±0.1 % over 10,000,000 windows, with the sum conserved by construction and no zero-weight failure mode.
- Results are reproducible (fixed seed) and displayed per decile in the interface with this date.
